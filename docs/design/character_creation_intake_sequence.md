# Character Creation Intake Sequence

**Status:** Narrative draft / design handoff  
**Draft date:** 27 November 2026  
**Scope:** Narrative content and intended data mapping. No mechanics are finalized here.

## Design intent

The intake sequence creates a person through institutional questioning, not a character-sheet questionnaire. A single compound intake clerk asks eight stages in order. Every stage is an independent choice; odd combinations are recorded and reacted to, never blocked.

The player chooses he, she, or they before the scene. The player types a name. The clerk records it with an institutional designation derived from Origin (place, parentage, or role). Correction is possible later at high cost, but price and mechanism remain undecided.

Formative contains two memories: one chosen, potentially skill-affinity-bearing memory, and one clerk-pried vulnerability memory. At Instruction, the mentor offers a blank clay tablet and says: “Make your mark.”

The four Paths are Commercial, Martial, Civic, and Religious. Identity is revealed through a scene, not self-description. The creation Biography is explicitly game narration. A later Archival Reconstruction must be built only from records that actually exist. The clerk closes by speaking a count of irregularities.

## Historical cautions

- Proto-cuneiform around 3200 BCE is represented as logograms, numerals, and functional designations—not prose. No one writes or reads paragraphs in-world.
- Arbitrary personal names may not be phonetic spellings; records may identify people by place, parentage, or role. Validate the naming/designation language before final VO.
- The late Uruk economy is institutional and ration-based. Silver as standard payment and private moneylending may be anachronistic; creditors should be households or institutions.
- Whether women held administrative posts at this date is uncertain. The narrative does not depend on it.
- Mentor profiles originate in earlier drafts and UrukSeeder; verify them against the current repository before treating them as canonical.

## Clerk

Male, ink-stained, not cruel. Each answer is one he may have to defend upward. He speaks in short lines, remembers earlier answers aloud, records what he hears, and never forbids a choice. Spoken dialogue must be speakable—not written-record language.

## Opening

Before dawn in the cool storehouse yard. Wet clay, spilled barley, sour beer. The clerk presses a reed into damp clay beside finished tablets. The player cannot read the marks but can see one concerns them.

**Clerk:** “Stand there. I ask, you answer, and I write down whichever of those two I find more useful. Don’t take it personally. I do it to everyone.”

Pronoun selection happens before this scene, outside the fiction.

## Stage 1 — Origins

**Clerk:** “Name the place first. Not the family. Families lie. I can check places.”

1. **A ward inside the city walls**  
   Clerk: “City-born. Good. You’ll know which gate the grain comes in by, and so will everyone who might hold that against you.”  
   Fragment: Born within the walls, where every neighbor knows whose child this is and what the family did last winter.  
   Teeth: family history arrives before the player does.

2. **The southern marsh-fringe**  
   Clerk: “Marsh. The dry ground here will feel very dry.”  
   Fragment: From the marsh-fringe, where tithes are paid in reed-bundles and the city is something that happens from a distance.  
   Teeth: clerks hear the marsh in the player’s speech before their arithmetic.

3. **A barley village on the canals**  
   Clerk: “Barley people. Then you know what a short measure costs, and who actually pays for it.”  
   Fragment: Raised on the irrigated plain, in a house that measured its year by what the storehouse took.  
   Teeth: countryside knowledge makes some quota verdicts hard to sign.

4. **A herding camp on the desert edge**  
   Clerk: “Flock-people. You’re in none of my registers. I’ll have to start you a page.”  
   Fragment: Of the herding camps, known to the city only as wool and mutton, and entered in no record before this one.  
   Teeth: no paper trail or nearby witness.

5. **A trading town far from the plain**  
   Clerk: “Foreign. I’ll write what I hear. If I hear it wrong, that’s the spelling you live with.”  
   Fragment: A stranger to the plain, recorded as the clerk heard the name, not quite as it was said.  
   Teeth: any spelling error propagates through later records.

## Stage 2 — Responsibilities

**Clerk:** “Everyone drags something through that gate. Tell me what’s tied to your ankle.”

1. **A grain debt** (institutional pressure, no named person)  
   Clerk: “The storehouse already has this one on a tablet. You may as well tell me the number.”  
   Fragment: Arrived owing a seed-loan unpaid across two sowings, a debt the storehouse knew before a word was spoken.  
   Teeth: the institution holds the household’s debt.

2. **Mouths that depend on you** (institutional pressure, no named person)  
   Clerk: “Who eats if you do well here? Who eats if you don’t?”  
   Fragment: Carries an aging parent and younger siblings, whose rations depend on this post.  
   Teeth: ration choices have faces behind them.

3. **A household you owe** (named person/household to author)  
   Clerk: “A house lent your people grain, or a boat, or a season of work. It wants more than the loan back. Houses do.”  
   Fragment: Indebted to a household that expects repayment in favors as well as grain.  
   Teeth: someone may ask for a small falsehood.

4. **A kinsman’s missing obligation** (named person to author)  
   Clerk: “Someone in your family owed the quay for a cargo that never arrived. Where is that person?” He does not wait. “The name is next to yours in my head already.”  
   Fragment: Answers for a kinsman’s shortfall, and for the kinsman, who vanished with it.  
   Teeth: the kinsman may return.

5. **Nothing**  
   Clerk: “No debts. No dependents. No quarrel with anyone.” He sets down the reed. “You’re the only person today who walked in clean. I don’t like it.”  
   Fragment: Owed nothing and owing nothing, recorded as a puzzle rather than a virtue.  
   Teeth: no pressure, no cushion, and clerkly suspicion.

## Stage 3 — Formative

**Clerk:** “Every scribe points at one day. The day you understood that a mark on clay could do what a hand couldn’t. Give me yours.”

1. **The day a count was wrong** (arithmetic affinity)  
   A quay tally cost someone their place; the player had counted the baskets. Fragment: Learned early that a number can ruin a person, having counted the baskets personally on the day one did. Teeth: the player was right; clerk asks whether they spoke or stayed silent.

2. **The day you read what you shouldn’t have** (literacy affinity)  
   A visiting official left a tablet unattended; the player puzzled out marks not meant for them. Fragment: Taught the marks from a tablet left unguarded, and has kept what it said ever since. Teeth: improperly learned knowledge may be discovered.

3. **The day a boundary dispute was settled** (law affinity)  
   A household lost land it rightly held because the other side had a tablet. Fragment: Watched a household lose land it rightly held because the other side had a tablet and theirs did not. Teeth: truth without a document can be powerless.

4. **The day the storehouse nearly ran out** (stewardship affinity)  
   The player watched ration priority in a bad season. Fragment: Saw what a short season does to a storehouse, and which names were last on the ration. Teeth: scarcity always forces a choice.

5. **The day you saw what a seal could do** (intrigue affinity)  
   A pressed mark changed whose grain was whose. Fragment: Understood in one afternoon that the mark pressed in the clay matters more than whoever pressed it. Teeth: the player knows how little it takes to change a record.

### Stage 3b — The one you’d rather not tell me

This memory adds vulnerability only; no skill.

**Clerk:** “Now the one you’d rather not tell me. I’ll write it down either way. You may as well choose the words.”

1. **You took something that wasn’t yours.** Grain, a seal-blank, or a token; someone else took blame. Fragment: Took what was not to be taken, and let another bear the blame.
2. **You saw a wrong and said nothing.** Short count, pressed seal, or beating. Fragment: Witnessed a wrong and kept quiet, and remembers who saw the silence.
3. **You gave a name.** An official asked about a neighbor; the player never learned what happened. Fragment: Gave a neighbor’s name to an official, and never learned what became of the neighbor.
4. **You left someone behind.** A sibling or friend wanted the place the player took. Fragment: Took the place that was offered and left behind the one who had hoped for it.
5. **Nothing.** Clerk: “Everyone says nothing. I’ll write ‘nothing,’ and I’ll remember you said it.” Fragment: Declined a second account, which the clerk recorded as one.

## Stage 4 — Entry

**Clerk:** “How did you come through that gate? Not why. How. The how decides who owns the first season of your life.”

1. **Debt pledge**  
   Clerk: “So you’re collateral. Collateral is safe as long as the debt stays unpaid. Think about what that means for the people who hold it.”  
   Fragment: Entered as collateral against a debt, and is worth more to the compound while the debt stays unpaid.  
   Teeth: the compound has reason to keep the player owing.

2. **Temple draft**  
   Clerk: “Levied. The temple has first claim on you and will remember it chose you.”  
   Fragment: Drawn into the compound by temple levy, and answerable to the temple before anyone else.  
   Teeth: the temple claims the player regardless of lane.

3. **Family placement**  
   Clerk: “Bought in. Then someone expects a return on the purchase, and it isn’t me.”  
   Fragment: Placed by family, who count what they spent.  
   Teeth: family watches productivity.

4. **Spoken for** (proposal)  
   Clerk: “Spoken for. By whom?” He waits and writes whichever answer comes.  
   Fragment: Put forward by an official whose reasons were never explained.  
   Teeth: that official’s enemies become the player’s.

5. **Walked in** (proposal)  
   Clerk: “No pledge, no levy, no sponsor. I should send you away.” He does not. “I’m curious how long you last.”  
   Fragment: Entered unsummoned and tolerated, which is not the same as belonging.  
   Teeth: no one has reason to protect the player.

Combination example: Stage 2 grain debt + Stage 4 debt pledge: “Pledged against the same debt? Then the storehouse owns both ends of you.”

## Stage 5 — Instruction

**Clerk:** “I can’t teach you where the lines go. I only write them down. Three names are open. Who are you lodging under?”

- **Shuruppak-ilu** (senior scribe, west wing archives)  
  Clerk: “West wing archives. He won’t shout. He’ll point his reed at your mistake and wait until you hear yourself being wrong.”  
  Fragment: Instructed under the dry hand of Shuruppak-ilu, who valued an honest gap in the record above a tidy lie.  
  Scene: blank clay tablet, dry reed, waiting. “Make your mark.”

- **Utu-mansum** (tax scribe, the gate)  
  Clerk: “The gate. Whatever crosses that sill passes him first. He smells of river dust and stale beer, and he’s never once been rushed.”  
  Fragment: Lodged under Utu-mansum at the gate, where every mark had to be defended to an angry boatman.  
  Scene: blank tablet on the scale. “Make your mark.”

- **Ur-Nanna** (temple steward, Storehouse Bay Four; optional third mentor)  
  Clerk: “Storehouse Bay Four. He listens to the weather the way other men listen to a creditor. He’ll be glad of company when the roof gives.”  
  Fragment: Taken in by Ur-Nanna, who taught how to keep a storehouse from rotting from the inside, and to share the blame when it did.  
  Scene: tablet handed over while he glances at the sky. “Make your mark.”

- **Unlodged** (proposal)  
  Clerk: “Neither? Then the first man who wants you gets to name the price.”  
  Fragment: Entered without a teacher, and owes the first lessons to whoever asked for the most.

## Stage 6 — Path

**Clerk:** “A scribe is never just a scribe. Which door do I write you through?”

- **Commercial:** cargo, vessels, cloth; every count has someone who wants it changed. Fragment: Set to the Commercial lane, among consignments and the people who would like them counted differently. Teeth: merchants always want something.
- **Martial:** watch and stores, bronze and those who carry it. Fragment: Set to the Martial lane, among stores of bronze and those who carry it. Teeth: figures decide who is armed.
- **Civic:** fields, labor, ration lines. Fragment: Set to the Civic lane, among fields, labor rolls, and ration lines. Teeth: the player tells owed people no.
- **Religious:** offerings and allotments. Fragment: Set to the Religious lane, among offerings and allotments, assumed to answer to something higher. Teeth: people expect piety and notice its absence.

**Friction rule:** never gate a combination; clerk comments dryly. Example: temple levy + Martial: “A temple levy heading for the armory. The priests won’t like it. The watch takes anyone who can count bronze without losing a finger. I’ll note it down.” Watch/armory institution names are placeholders pending Era I Martial families.

## Stage 7 — Identity

**Clerk:** “Your name. Say it.” Player types the name. Clerk records it with the Origin designation, e.g. “Kullani. Of the marsh-fringe. That’s how the register will know you.”

Proposed designations: of the walls; of the marsh-fringe; of the canal barley; of the herding camps; the outlander. Only foreign-town Origin may alter name spelling.

Then the scene: a carrier drops wet, rotten, short-count grain. “Write it sound,” the carrier says. Clerk: “Well?”

1. **Write it as it is, rotten and short, with the carrier’s name beside it.** Trait: exacting. Fragment: Marked by the clerk as exacting, writing what is seen and carrying the cost of it. Teeth: enemies among those expecting a bend.
2. **Write it sound and say nothing.** Trait: pliable. Fragment: Marked as pliable, writing what the person in front of the desk requires. Teeth: people seeking bent records will remember.
3. **Call the guard and hold the carrier until the count is settled.** Trait: severe. Fragment: Marked as severe, quick to bring force to a disputed count. Teeth: fear is not cooperation.
4. **Hold the reed and send for the senior scribe before writing.** Trait: cautious. Fragment: Marked as cautious, committing nothing to clay until someone senior has seen it. Teeth: slowness is noticed.

## Stage 8 — Biography

Creation Biography is game narration, not a record written or read in-world. String selected fragments in stage order, with name/designation. Example:

> Kullani, of the marsh-fringe. From the marsh-fringe, where tithes are paid in reed-bundles and the city is something that happens from a distance. Arrived owing a seed-loan unpaid across two sowings, a debt the storehouse knew before a word was spoken. Learned early that a number can ruin a person, having counted the baskets personally on the day one did. Gave a neighbor’s name to an official, and never learned what became of the neighbor. Drawn into the compound by temple levy, and answerable to the temple before anyone else. Instructed under the dry hand of Shuruppak-ilu, who valued an honest gap in the record above a tidy lie. Set to the Martial lane, among stores of bronze and those who carry it. Marked as cautious, committing nothing to clay until someone senior has seen it.

Then clerk speaks from memory, not tablet. Irregularity bands are illustrative:
- **0:** “Clean. I don’t trust it.”
- **1–2:** “Ordinary. Don’t let it go to your head.”
- **3–4:** “That’s three things that don’t fit. Most manage two.”
- **5+:** “I’ve run out of columns.”

Example irregularities: temple levy in the armory; marsh-born hand among spears; a name given away. Final line: “Stand there. Your mark is in the register. Don’t touch it.”

## Later Archival Reconstruction

Build only from persisted records. It should be silent about private memories and unrecorded choices. Sample:

> Kullani, of the marsh-fringe. Lodged under Shuruppak-ilu. Assigned to the Martial lane. A debt entered against the household. The records are silent on the rest.

The difference between the game-narrated Biography and the archival reconstruction is intentional.

## Appendix A — First workday mentor lines

These belong in Turn 1, **The First Workday**, not in the intake interview. Mentor profiles are unverified; check against repo before using.

- **Shuruppak-ilu:** “Sit. The reed is crooked in your hand, and your sums are soft at the edges. Fix the arithmetic on the third column, and don’t invent numbers to make the clay look happy.”
- **Utu-mansum:** “Stand by the scale. Don’t look at the sky, don’t talk to the boatmen, and don’t let anyone rush your tally. What crosses that sill belongs to the temple until I say otherwise.”

## Implementation handoff (not mechanics approval)

The sequence should map onto existing systems without engine rewrite:
- Stages 1–6 populate creation choices, PersonEntity components/skills, and initial relationship/pressure flags.
- Formative's first memory may point to a skill affinity; Stage 3b is vulnerability/narrative only.
- Stage 7 applies one registered core trait: exacting, pliable, severe, or cautious.
- Stage 8 compiles selected fragments into PersonEntity.memory and initializes an irregularity/audit value on WorldState only after the counting rule is decided.
- Appendix A fires on Turn 1 / **The First Workday**, delivering the selected mentor’s line at the cedar desk.

Use existing entities, registries, and save architecture. Do not create a parallel character model or a new trait/personality subsystem. Keep narrative fragments in data; composition and validation in code. No engine rewrite is needed.
